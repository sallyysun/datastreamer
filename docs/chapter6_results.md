## Set-up in one paragraph

Five fixed memories M ∈ {39, 99, 199, 399, 1999} per estimator (Xiao, Hermite, MPL), on shared
rank-PIT inputs (window 500). Banks: Hedge + fixed share over the five arms, scored from tick 30.
**Squared bank** η = 1, δ = 1e-4 (loss (p̂ − ρ)², p̂ = 12(û − ½)(v̂ − ½)); **log bank** η = 1, δ = 3e-4
(Gaussian method-of-moments log density). Uniform = the same arms at weight 1/5. VFF = each
estimator's own variable-forgetting rule, started at M = 199, at rate 0.05 (slow) or 0.5 (fast);
the better rate is picked per cell in hindsight. Families: Gaussian, Clayton, Gumbel, t₄.

## Criterion

The thesis cares about **drift** and **jump**. A practitioner does not know in advance which is
coming, so the bank succeeds if it beats **any single fixed memory or VFF** one could commit to
beforehand. The memory that is best in hindsight for each cell is a ceiling, not the target.
The single memory with the best mean over drift + jump is **M = 199** for every family and estimator.
`constant` and `oscillate` are controls only; they were not used for tuning.

## Tuning (seeds 1–30)

Objective: whole-run RMSE minus the hindsight-best fixed memory, averaged over drift + jump, all
families and estimators; one (η, δ) per loss. Grid: η ∈ {0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16} (log from
0.25), δ ∈ {0, 1e-5, 3e-5, 1e-4, 3e-4, 4e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3, 1e-2}. The arms do not depend on (η, δ),
so each seed runs them once and replays the weight recursion; at the old values this reproduces
`probe_thesis_bank` exactly.

| Bank | old (η, δ): mean regret ×10⁻³ | tuned (η, δ): mean regret ×10⁻³ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| squared | (0.5, 1e-4): −1.2 | **(1, 1e-4): −3.3** |
| log | (1, 4e-4): −6.6 | **(1, 3e-4): −6.8** |

- Flat near the optimum: for the log bank, η ∈ [0.5, 2] × δ ∈ [1e-5, 1e-3] is within 2 of the best.
- Same optimum for every estimator (η = 1; δ = 1e-4 or 3e-4).
- η ≥ 4 with δ > 0 is unstable (+20 to +70: single-tick noise flips the weights); with δ = 0 it locks on one arm.
- Log bank: RMSE optimum = log-loss optimum. Squared bank: η = 1 gains ~2 in RMSE over η = 0.5 but
  loses ~2 ×10⁻³ nats/tick in log loss.

## Relation to the Chapter 5 bounds (§5.3.4, thesis v5)

- **Log bank:** η = 1 is the value at which §5.3.4's log-loss bounds hold, with the bank read as
  the mixture of its arms' copulas. Its ρ output Σ w·ρ_k is the Spearman's ρ of that mixture, because
  ρ_S is linear in C. With K = 5, δ = 3e-4 and T ≈ 4970 scored ticks: B_{T,0} ≈ 2.8 and
  B_{T,1} ≈ 12.5 nats over the whole run. The bound is on the mixture's log loss, not on RMSE.
- **Squared bank:** not covered. Exp-concavity needs η ≤ 1/32, and the tuned η is 1. On held-out
  jump at δ = 1e-4 (mean over families and estimators, against the hindsight-best memory):
  η = 0.1 gives +2.6 and η = 1 gives −11.8 ×10⁻³. Small η gives up the adaptation.

## VFF caveats

- Hermite VFF is inert: within 0.1 ×10⁻³ of fixed M = 199 everywhere.
- MPL VFF: slow rate is +17 to +33 behind the best fixed M on drift, within ±6 on jump; fast rate collapses to the shortest memory.
- Only Xiao's VFF adapts meaningfully. It is the best method on Gaussian drift (−0.6 ± 0.4 vs the best arm); on jump it gains at most 2.8 over the best fixed M while the log bank gains a further 12–20.
- t₄ jump: fast VFF is best after the change (53.1 vs 83.7 for the log bank) and worst before it (154.4 vs 48.0).
- Squared bank's excess log loss with Hermite on jump is inflated by a few seeds (Gaussian: 24.8 mean, SD 28, vs 8.8 for the log bank). This already happened before tuning (21.7).
