# Results, 2026-09-22 — the parametric arm, the rate budget, and regret

Branch `adaptive-memory`. Everything below is measured; §-references point at the notes that
carry the protocol and the caveats.

**What was added.** Three things, none of which needed a new estimator:

1. The **parametric arm** (`mle_ew`) joined both the memory sweep and the adaptive probe — it had
   been missing from both, so the thesis looked like Xiao + Hermite + a Kalman reference.
2. The **rate budget** experiment `copula_v2.md` §8.4 specified but never ran.
3. **Regret** as an evaluation lens: minimax, switching, and a time decomposition.

## 1. The optimal memory is a property of the target, not the estimator

*(`memory_sweep.md` §9.1)*

| scenario / window | best memory, Xiao | best memory, MLE | MLE vs Xiao at matched memory |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `constant` | 30 000 | 9 793 | −1.7 % (t = −0.2, tie) |
| `drift` | 235 | **235** | −6.1 % (t = −5.8) |
| `oscillate` | 36 | **36** | −5.8 % (t = −11.6) |
| `jump` all | 162 | **162** | −6.9 % (t = −8.8) |
| `jump` settled | 495 | **495** | −32.4 % (t = −7.6) |
| `jump` recovery | 53 | 36 | −14.2 % (t = −9.8) |

**Xiao and the MLE pick the same memory in 20 of 24 cells**, across four DGP families.

**Read it as:** the lag term $d\,M/2$ doesn't depend on how you represent dependence, and the
variance term enters the optimum only as a cube root — so a 1.6× variance difference between
estimators moves $M^*$ by 17 %, which is inside one grid step. This is what makes a single
"memory axis" legitimate, and it is now demonstrated rather than argued.

**How the MLE got onto that axis.** It has no visible λ; its memory hides in
$H_t = \lambda H_{t-1} + I_\theta$, whose steady state $H \to I_\theta/(1-\lambda)$ makes the
Fisher step $(1-\lambda)s_\theta/I_\theta$ — an exponential average with $\alpha = 1-\lambda$.
Verified against a measured step response: half-life ratio **0.955**, against Xiao's 0.993.

## 2. The parametric edge is payment for knowing the family

*(`memory_sweep.md` §9.2)*

The sweep's DGP was hardcoded Gaussian — which is the Gaussian-copula MLE's own family.

| DGP | MLE beats Xiao in | worst cell |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **gaussian** (correctly specified) | **6 of 6** | — |
| gumbel | 5 of 6 | +14.2 % |
| clayton | 3 of 6 | **+66.7 %** (t = 8.8) |
| student-t | 1 of 6 | +10.0 % (t = 12.1) |

**Read it as:** on-family the MLE wins everything; off-family it is **9–9 across 18 cells**, with
a worst case of +66.7 %. You are risking 67 % to gain 6 %, and on real data nobody hands you the
copula family. **Xiao is the only arm never badly wrong in any family.**

That is the argument for the nonparametric route — and the reason to keep the parametric arm in
the thesis, because it *demonstrates* the risk instead of asserting it.

## 3. When you cannot keep up, remember longer — by a cube root

*(`rate_budget.md`)*

If the estimator consumes only a fraction $f$ of arriving ticks, the truth keeps moving anyway,
so an ESS of $M$ observations spans $M/f$ wall-clock ticks:

$$\mathrm{MSE}(M_{\text{ticks}}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{f\,M_{\text{ticks}}}
+ \left(\frac{d\,M_{\text{ticks}}}{2}\right)^{2}
\;\Longrightarrow\; M^*_{\text{ticks}} \propto f^{-1/3}$$

| `drift` | measured | predicted |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| optimal memory, $f = 1/2$ | **1.25×** | 1.26× |
| optimal memory, $f = 1/4$ | **1.67×** | 1.59× |
| RMSE cost, $f = 1/2$ | **+26 %** | +26 % |
| RMSE cost, $f = 1/4$ | **+56 %** | +59 % |

**Read it as:** the rate budget is not a systems footnote — it is the same U-curve pushed from
the variance side. And the *same* cube root turned out to govern the cost, which was not designed
in: $\mathrm{RMSE}^* \propto f^{-1/3}$ too.

**Two caveats.** `jump` overshoots (1.4× and 2.0×), because a step is not the linear drift the
algebra assumes — the law holds where its assumption holds. And **thinning beats batching**
everywhere by 1.3–8.4 %, which is the *opposite* of what §8.4 conjectured: batching goes blind
for 15 ticks in 20, and that staleness costs more than within-block smearing saves.

## 4. The bank is the minimax-regret policy

*(`adaptive_memory.md` §12)*

§3 of that note reports the bank beating `known_best` on `jump` only and losing 8.9–21.7σ
elsewhere. That comparison faces **four different opponents, each already told which scenario is
running** — information no deployable estimator has. `known_best` is not a policy; it's an oracle.

Against the best loss achievable per scenario, worst case over all four (×10⁻³ MSE):

| family | bank | best fixed memory | bank is |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `xiao` | **2.50** | 10.37 | **4.2×** better |
| `hermite` | **2.36** | 11.91 | **5.0×** better |
| `hermite_db` | **2.33** | 11.75 | **5.0×** better |
| `mle` | **2.50** | 8.88 | **3.6×** better |
| `kalman` | **4.77** | 18.78 | **3.9×** better |

**The bank is rank 1 of 14–23 candidate policies in all five families.** It is never best on any
single scenario (except `jump`, where its regret is 0) and never far from best on any.

**Why regret and not loss.** Minimax *loss* is decided entirely by the hardest scenario:

| `xiao` | `constant` | `oscillate` | max loss | max regret |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `arm0` (25 t) | 95 865 | 166 266 | **166 266** | **124 308** |
| `bank` | 4 105 | 161 467 | **161 467** | **3 303** |

By max loss, a 25-tick fixed memory looks 3 % off the bank. It is in fact 44× worse than
achievable on `constant`, and max loss cannot see that because 95 865 is below its own
`oscillate` number. Subtracting $L^*(e)$ is what stops the hardest scenario swallowing the
comparison.

**Read it as:** the damaging summary ("loses everywhere but `jump`") is still true and was never
the decision-relevant question. Nothing in the numbers changed; the opponent did.

## 5. Switching regret: the bank beats a fixed memory, and loses to one well-placed switch

*(`adaptive_memory.md` §13)*

The comparator that actually tests a bank is the best *sequence* of memories with at most $K$
switches, solved exactly by a DP over (arm, switches). On `jump` (×10⁻³):

| family | bank | $L^*(0)$ | $L^*(1)$ | regret vs $K{=}0$ | **capture** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `xiao` | 26 965 | 28 846 | 15 669 | **−1 881** | 14.3 % |
| `hermite_db` | 29 205 | 31 088 | 19 901 | **−1 883** | 16.8 % |
| `mle` | 23 075 | 24 966 | 11 439 | **−1 891** | 14.0 % |
| `kalman` | 20 821 | 32 911 | 18 257 | **−12 090** | **82.5 %** |

Capture $= (L^*(0) - L_{\text{bank}}) / (L^*(0) - L^*(1))$ — the fraction of a single switch's
value the bank actually takes.

**Read it as:** the $K{=}1$ oracle switches at tick 2500 exactly — the jump tick — so it is *told*
when the change happens. No online method can reach it; it is a bound, not a rival. The
informative comparison is bank against bank: **both are blind, and one takes 82 % while the others
take 14 %.** That gap is an implementation difference, not missing information.

## 6. ⚠️ The bank earns before the change and pays during it

*(`adaptive_memory.md` §15, Figures 3 and 4)*

The same per-tick losses, decomposed by window (×10⁻³, negative = ahead of the best fixed memory):

| policy | pre | **recovery** | settled | total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `bank` | **−7 647** | **+4 860** | +905 | −1 881 |
| `bank8_steered` | −7 932 | +4 355 | +504 | −3 074 |
| `bank_eta4` | −6 006 | **−1 561** | −125 | −7 692 |
| `vff_fast` | −1 223 | **+27 815** | −1 011 | +25 581 |

**This is the most uncomfortable result of the day.** The bank's whole-run win of −1 881 is
$-7647 + 4860 + 905$. It is earned entirely on the stationary stretch *before* the jump, and part
of it is given back during the recovery window the device was supposed to be for.

The mechanism: by t = 2500 the posterior has settled on a long arm, which beats a fixed compromise
memory comfortably on stationary data. Then the change arrives and it spends ~200 ticks on that
stale memory — exactly where squared error is largest.

**Same shape in all three families.** Only `bank_eta4` has negative recovery regret, i.e. actually
wins at the change; §7 below prices what that costs.

**Why RMSE does not show this.** RMSE *can* be sliced by window and does show the sign. What it
gets wrong is the size: by RMSE ratio `settled` (1.23) looks like a worse failure than `recovery`
(1.14), when recovery costs 4 860 and settled costs 905 — **5× more damaging, ranked backwards**.
RMSE divides by window length and takes a square root, both of which shrink a short high-error
stretch. And RMSE is not additive: you cannot write the whole run as a sum of its windows.

## 7. Splitting selection from estimation

*(`adaptive_memory.md` §16)*

Selecting memory and estimating ρ are different skills, and no family is good at both:
`KalmanBank` captures 63–82 % of a one-switch oracle and has the worst minimax regret of any
family; Xiao has the best minimax regret and its bank captures 14 %.

`kalman_steered.hpp` runs the bank purely as a **selector** — it never reports ρ — feeding its
posterior-mean memory to one Xiao histogram through the $\lambda = 1-K$ identity.

| `xiao` | `constant` | `drift` | `jump` | `oscillate` | worst regret | capture | store |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `bank` (7 arms) | 4 105 | **22 517** | 26 965 | 161 467 | **3 303** | 14.3 % | 52 KB |
| `steered` alone | **2 435** | 23 421 | 25 898 | 166 817 | 6 364 | 22.4 % | **5.9 KB** |
| **`bank8_steered`** | 3 704 | 22 601 | **25 773** | **161 264** | 3 387 | **23.3 %** | 58 KB |

- **`steered` alone**: 41 % better on `constant`, 8.8× cheaper, and worse on `oscillate` — a
  failure predicted before the build, because a 20-tick block cannot resolve a 400-tick cycle.
- **`bank8_steered`** puts the steered estimator in as an **eighth arm** and lets the bank's own
  likelihood weigh it. Best on `jump` *and* `oscillate`, worst-case regret 2.5 % off the plain
  bank's, capture 14.3 % → **23.3 %** — better than the steered arm reaches alone.

**Read it as:** the principled way to say "use it when it helps" is an arm, not a detector. A
detector keyed to `oscillate` would improve the minimax number *by construction*, because
`oscillate` is one of the four scenarios that define the evaluation.

⚠️ It does **not** fix the storage complaint — it worsens it, 58 KB against 52 KB. Tracking,
robustness and cost have no common optimum here.

## 8. Refuted today

Three predictions of mine, all measured and all wrong. Recorded because the thesis keeps a
retraction table.

| claim | what happened |
| --- | --- |
| **Per-arm predictive variance** is the 68-point gap between the generic banks and `KalmanBank` (§13.2) | Worth **+2 points**, not 68. Kept as an off-by-default option; it is not the fix. |
| **A faster learning rate** ($\eta$) is an improvement | It is a **trade**. $\eta = 4$ takes capture 14 % → 58 % and makes worst-case regret **13× worse**, paid on `oscillate`. The shipped defaults are already near minimax-optimal. |
| **A shorter selector block** fixes `steered` on `oscillate` | Makes it **worse**. $\sigma_v^2 = 1.06/(m-3)$, so halving the block pays 3.4× the noise for the resolution it buys. |

**One knob, two jobs — the third sighting.** The VFF's $r$ is smoothing rate *and* step size.
Memory $M$ is variance *and* lag. The selector's block $m$ is resolution *and* noise. This pattern
is worth naming explicitly in the thesis.

**One good-news control:** randomising the change time per seed costs almost nothing
(−16.2 % → −8.3 % capture), so the harness is not trivially memorisable. But the **scoring window**
does almost everything — narrowed around the change, the generic banks' capture goes *negative*,
while `KalmanBank` stays at 63 %. §13.1's number is window-dependent and now says so.

## 9. What this says about the thesis

- **Memory is the common problem**, and that is now a measurement (§1) rather than a framing
  choice: four representations, one parametric, agree on the optimum in 20 of 24 cells.
- **Adaptive memory is worth having**, on the criterion a practitioner faces (§4) — 3.6–5.0×
  better worst case than any fixed memory, in every family.
- **But it works for a different reason than advertised** (§6): it tracks stationary stretches
  better than a fixed compromise, rather than reacting quickly at changes. Reacting quickly is a
  separate setting, it works, and it costs more than it returns (§8).

That three-part claim is more useful than "adaptive memory wins", and it is what the data supports.

**Suggested framing:** *In addition to estimation accuracy and recovery time, we evaluate
cumulative estimation regret relative to fixed- and switching-memory benchmarks. This provides a
complementary view of the stationary-efficiency–adaptation tradeoff by quantifying the cumulative
cost of imperfect memory selection before, during and after dependence changes.*

## 10. Open, in the order I would do them

1. **Cite MMAE and fixed-share.** The bank is Magill (1965) multiple-model adaptive estimation,
   and its mixing step is Herbster & Warmuth (1998) fixed-share — which gives $p$ a theoretical
   meaning and the bank a switching-regret bound. This is the highest-risk gap in the writing.
2. **Real data.** Everything is synthetic, five hand-built scenarios. One real pair showing the
   bank's memory trajectory, with no RMSE claim attached, would change how the work reads.
3. **Per-window tables for `hermite_db` and `mle`.** The curves exist; the tables don't.
4. **Not worth doing:** a learned memory policy. An oracle allowed to switch between bank settings
   at hindsight-chosen moments gains **7.9–10.5 % on `jump` and ~0 % on the other three**. That is
   the ceiling, and it is below the cost of the infrastructure.
